## Users
Primarily Ethan himself: a personal corner of the internet for writing, experiments, and showing work. Secondary visitors are curious developers, friends, and the occasional recruiter who arrive from a link, skim a post or a project, and leave. They are relaxed and exploring, usually on a laptop in the evening or a phone on the couch.

## Product Purpose
ethrmor.com is a personal site and blog for Ethan Morris, a web developer. It holds featured projects, a short about/work history, and posts about front-end craft (CSS, interactions, small experiments). Success is a site Ethan is proud to keep adding to, where posts and projects get equal weight and the site itself demonstrates the craft it writes about.

## Brand Personality
Calm, crafted, quietly playful, and specific to Ethan: hockey (rink markings, hockey-card projects, player profile) and family over generic developer tropes. Palette: ice white, near-black, Blackhawks red, with rink-blue only in markings. Subtle and modern: generous space and large confident type like Apple product pages, small hand-tuned interactive details like personal dev sites (Josh Comeau, Rauno Freiberg), and the soft depth and rich hover states of Raycast / Arc. Voice is first-person, friendly, and plain-spoken.

## Anti-references
- Faint background grid patterns and other "developer template" decoration.
- Astro / Next.js starter-template look: centered column, default blog list, placeholder hero images.
- Glass cards everywhere, gradient text, neon glows, busy multi-accent palettes.
- Section eyebrows / numbered section labels as scaffolding.
- Anything loud: the personality lives in details, not volume.

## Design Principles
0. Human curated, never AI generated (the top priority). Every element should look chosen by a person: honest content (real projects with real status, including unfinished ones), uneven emphasis where it is earned instead of symmetric grids, specific copy in Ethan's voice instead of generic taglines, and small personal artifacts (what he's working on now, a colophon) over decoration. If a section could appear unchanged on any other developer's site, rewrite it.
1. Practice what you preach: the site is itself a demonstration of the front-end craft the blog writes about.
2. Quiet surface, rich details: restraint at a glance, delight on interaction (hover, focus, transitions).
3. Type carries the hierarchy: scale and weight do the work instead of boxes and borders.
4. Content first, equal weight: projects and posts are both first-class; nothing is filler.
5. Every theme is a first-class theme: light and dark get the same care.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 body contrast in both themes, visible focus states, keyboard-reachable everything. Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback. Theme follows the system setting with a manual override.
